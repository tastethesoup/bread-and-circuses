Good morning. It's Friday, November 13th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Here's the strange thing about Thursday. Nvidia's co-founder left as chief executive. The stock fell 14 percent at the open, per Bloomberg. And the bonds barely moved. Barron's put it plainly: "Credit spreads barely budged after Huang's departure." The cost of insuring Nvidia's debt for five years ended the day roughly flat, around 140 basis points.

Meanwhile, the supposedly safe asset sold off. The ten-year Treasury closed at 5.10 percent, up 12 basis points from Tuesday. And a brand-new 30-year bond sold at 5.441 percent, the highest yield at a new 30-year's first auction since February 2001, per Capital Finance Bureau.

Bloomberg's Markets Daily headline this morning: "That Haven Trade in Treasury Bonds Didn't Last Long."

So that's our theme. Where's the haven? A question, not a claim. Nvidia's credit held while Treasuries wobbled. AI borrowers are looking for safety in collateral. And banks, per the Financial Times, are looking for it in insurance.

Also this morning: producer prices, a touch softer than expected but up 6.5 percent on the year. And Nick Timiraos on a rewrite of the Fed's 2027 calendar.
