Good morning. It's Friday, October 16th, and you are listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Yesterday I told you I was watching three things. All three got answered overnight.

One. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh took the stage in Bangkok last night and said nothing about interest rates. "I don't prejudge meetings," he said. Markets heard a hold. Per relays of futures pricing, the odds of an October hike fell to roughly 13 to 15 percent, from roughly 28 percent on Thursday.

Two. The spread data from Wednesday's AI credit rout are in, and they show the stress. The riskiest junk bonds, rated CCC and lower, hit a spread of 12.82 percent. That's a new high in the window I track. High yield widened to 325 basis points, the widest since the end of March.

Three. Volta, the roughly 5 billion dollar AI loan, got done. But it priced wide. Per Bloomberg, via public relays, it priced at SOFR plus 650 basis points, at 95 cents on the dollar. That's a yield above 11 percent.

Meanwhile, the bond rally kept going. The ten-year Treasury closed Thursday at 5.10 percent, its lowest close since September 22nd.
