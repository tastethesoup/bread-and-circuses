Good morning. It's Wednesday, November 18th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Yesterday I asked: if the AI story disappoints, who's holding the debt? Ask me again after Nvidia, I said. Well, before Nvidia even reported, the New York Fed's John Williams sharpened the question.

Speaking at an Axios event Tuesday afternoon, Williams said, quote, "You're looking for where's the leverage and where are the complex structures that maybe create unexpected disruptions in markets. But right now, we're not seeing that." And then: "We are definitely seeing structured finance of the type we saw in housing finance, so that's why we're paying attention to that."

A few hours later, Nvidia, which has backstopped a big piece of the AI buildout, told investors it will be, in its words, "disciplined and selective" in supporting the neocloud companies. It had 118 billion dollars of guarantees outstanding at the end of the quarter. And in Southeast Asia, per Bloomberg, bankers froze work on a 480 million dollar chip loan after a new US license rule.

So that's our theme. Where's the leverage? As the backstops get choosier, who ends up carrying the AI credit risk? A question, not a prediction.

Also this morning: Governor Waller says the Fed may need to "shock people" with a half-point hike. And the ten-year is still above 5.
