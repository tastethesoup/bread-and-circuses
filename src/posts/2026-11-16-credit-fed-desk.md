Good morning. It's Monday, November 16th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

The bond market just had its worst week since April of last year. Per Tradeweb data carried by Dow Jones, the ten-year yield rose 0.294 percentage point last week, to about 5.15 percent. That's the largest one-week gain since April 11th, 2025.

So who's driving that, and who pays? Over the weekend, some uncomfortable answers.

Natixis, in research written up in Bloomberg's Odd Lots newsletter, estimates that AI-related borrowing, including the interest-rate hedges on data-center loans, has pushed Treasury yields about 45 basis points higher this year. That's one bank's estimate. And in Bloomberg's Points of Return, Richard Abbey writes: "It looks ever more as though Big Tech is 'crowding out' the government and forcing the Treasury to offer higher rates."

So that's our theme. Crowded out? A question, not a claim. Because over the same stretch, junk-bond spreads got tighter, not wider. And one data-center borrower, QTS, cut its debt sale by about 60 percent rather than pay up.

Also this morning: Daly, Jefferson, Collins and Schmid all spoke after Friday's show, and all four put inflation first. Chair Warsh speaks at 11, after this show. And Nvidia reports tomorrow.
