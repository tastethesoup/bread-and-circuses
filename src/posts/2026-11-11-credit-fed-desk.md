Good morning. It's Wednesday, November 11th, Veterans Day. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

To everyone who has served, and to the families who served alongside you: thank you.

Here's this morning in one line, from Nick Timiraos of the Wall Street Journal, posted on X at 6:34 Central: "There is now little doubt that the Fed will raise rates in December. After a third firm inflation report, the question is how fast."

That's our theme. Not whether, but how fast.

Yesterday, the Fed's own officials gave three answers. Governor Christopher Waller, before the inflation data, put a half point on the table. After the data, Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the Fed needs "at least two more" quarter-point hikes, and "It may be possible to move more rapidly this winter." And in the afternoon, Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman said the benefit of more tightening "has to be carefully weighed against its costs."

The bond market gave its answer too. The two-year had its biggest one-day jump since April of last year. And the ten-year auction came in soft, at 5.02 percent.

In credit, CoreWeave said the quiet part out loud. Chief executive Mike Intrator, on last night's call, per MarketWatch: "We will not use the unsecured debt market to fund our business at current yields, nor do we need to." Same day, Bowman put a number on AI-related debt: on track to exceed 800 billion dollars this year.

The cash bond market is closed today for the holiday. Stocks are open.
