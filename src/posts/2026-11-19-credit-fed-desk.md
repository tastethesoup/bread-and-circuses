Good morning. It's Thursday, November 19th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Yesterday we asked, borrowing John Williams's words: where's the leverage? Yesterday afternoon, the Fed published the minutes of its October meeting. And the financial stability section reads almost like a reply.

Quote: "Most participants observed that AI-related debt had grown rapidly and that much of this growth had taken place through complex and opaque financing structures."

Complex and opaque. That's the Federal Open Market Committee talking, not a short seller.

Then, overnight, the Wall Street Journal reported that the 500 billion dollars Wall Street pledged in August to finance Nvidia's customers has deployed, in the Journal's words, "not a dollar." And at Bloomberg's credit forum in London, a Davidson Kempner partner said, "There's a real fear factor that developed in the market."

So that's our theme. If the structures are complex and opaque, who can actually see the risk? And who's still willing to fund it? A question, not a prediction.

Also this morning: the same minutes say almost all officials expect more hikes, and a couple floated more than a quarter point. And the 20-year auction was, in a word, average.
