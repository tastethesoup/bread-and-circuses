Good morning. It's Friday, October 9th, and you are listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

The week's bond scare ends with a shrug and a warning. The shrug: Thursday's 30-year auction cleared at 5.618 percent, and the trackers' verdict was "in line." Then, at 12:17 in the afternoon Eastern time, the President posted that the United States will not attack Iran before the midterm elections on November 3rd. The ten-year yield, which had spiked to around 5.32 percent in the morning, slid back to roughly 5.22 to 5.24 by the close. This morning it's at 5.25.

Now the warning. Pimco's chief investment officer says a 6 percent ten-year is "feasible." Mohamed El-Erian says the Treasury's effort to hold long yields down has stumbled. And the CCC spread jumped to 12.52 percent, up about 41 basis points in three sessions, while investment grade sat at 82, barely moving. Firmus pulled its IPO. And the White House has scheduled a closed hearing for Fed Governor Lisa Cook.
