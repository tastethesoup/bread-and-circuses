Good morning. It's Monday, November 23rd. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Over the weekend, Bloomberg's Points of Return newsletter quoted Anna Wu of Floodlines Research, and she summed up last week in three sentences. Quote: "The bond market and the Fed are not in full agreement. One of them could be making a mistake. In this case, I think it is the Fed, because the market may not be strong enough to take it this time."

That's our question today. Whose mistake? Not a prediction. A question.

The week in one breath. Junk bond spreads went from 323 basis points to 376. That's 53 wider in five trading days. The two-year Treasury yield went from 4.88 percent to 4.63, down 25. And the S&P 500 fell 8.6 percent on the week.

Now the split, in two lines. On Friday, Fed Governor Lisa Cook said financial conditions are "too accommodative," and that waiting "risks getting behind the curve." On Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said, quote, "Many people believe that they may be making a policy mistake if they over-tighten."

And this morning, futures are sharply higher. Just after 8 Central, per Reuters, S&P 500 futures were up about 4 percent. One good morning is not a verdict. It's one morning.
