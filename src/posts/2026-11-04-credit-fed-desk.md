Good morning. It's Wednesday, November 4th. It's the morning after the midterms, and it's refunding day. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Start with the vote, and I'll keep it strictly to markets. Based on media projections, Democrats won both the House and the Senate. Per Bloomberg, Treasuries rallied overnight, and the dollar had its biggest drop in two months. Stock futures were up about 1 percent this morning.

Then the borrowing. According to Reuters and Bloomberg reports, Meta priced 15 billion dollars of bonds, with a top coupon of 7.50 percent. At 7:30 this morning, Treasury kept its auction sizes unchanged. And at 7:15, ADP said private employers added 86,000 jobs.

Our theme today is paying up. Meta got its money, but it paid a lot more over Treasuries than it did in April. Some AI borrowers, per the Wall Street Journal, can't get a junk bond done at all right now. Bloomberg says the bond market rallied on likely limits to tax and spending plans. And workers who switch jobs are getting paid more, which keeps a December hike on the table.
