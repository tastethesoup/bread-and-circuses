Good morning. It's Tuesday, October 20th, and you are listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Yesterday, stocks bounced. The S and P 500 rose 0.41 percent. The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.11 percent.

Bonds did not join the party. The ten-year Treasury closed at 5.15 percent, up 6 basis points. That gives back part of last week's rally.

And Friday's junk-bond spreads are finally in. They widened again. High yield at 331 basis points. The riskiest bonds, rated CCC and lower, at 12.97 percent. That's a new high for the stretch I've been tracking.

This morning, the AI nerves are back. A South Korean data-center startup called Elice fell 32 percent on its first day of trading. The Kospi fell 3.7 percent. SoftBank's default insurance hit its highest level in about four years. And Nasdaq 100 futures are down about 0.4 percent.

In France, the ten-year yield is the highest since 2002, and the gap over Germany is the widest since 2012. Here at home, September housing starts came in down 5.2 percent, with a big asterisk I'll explain.

And John Authers of Bloomberg Opinion asks the question of the week. Is CoreWeave 2007, or is it 2023?
