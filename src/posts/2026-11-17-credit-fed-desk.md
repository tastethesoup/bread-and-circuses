Good morning. It's Tuesday, November 17th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Yesterday, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh read out three scenarios for the age of what he calls super intelligence, or SI. His staff built them, and he called them "illustrative scenarios, not forecasts." The third one goes like this: "Or SI could disappoint. After significant capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns could turn down and equity valuations could fall, leading to an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment."

That was Monday. The same day, stocks fell for a third straight session. US chipmakers dropped 5.3 percent, per Reuters. And Nvidia, which reports tonight, lost another 4.1 percent.

So that's our theme. If SI disappoints, who's holding the debt? A question, not a forecast. And the Fed just gave us a count. Its Financial Stability Report, out Friday, puts advanced-computing debt issuance at more than 680 billion dollars by October. A bank has sold a slice of a data-center construction loan in the mid-80s. And yet the junk-bond index barely moved.

Also this morning: St. Louis Fed President Musalem says the economy will likely need "several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027." Retail sales came in as expected, with a firmer core. And the ten-year is still above 5.
