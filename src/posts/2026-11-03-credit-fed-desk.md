Good morning. It's Tuesday, November 3rd. It's Election Day. And you are listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

At a summit in Hong Kong today, Pimco's chief executive, Emmanuel Roman, put some numbers on the AI borrowing boom. This is per CNBC. He said about 550 billion dollars of AI debt has already been issued this year, in various forms. He cited an estimate of about 10 trillion dollars of AI spending over five years. And then he said this: "At some point in time, it's going to be too much."

That's our theme today. How much is too much?

Because on Monday, the AI borrowers came right back. Oracle sold 10 billion dollars of bonds, with coupons as high as 8.65 percent. It also sold about 9.9 billion dollars of new stock, at 116 dollars a share. Per CNBC, that's roughly 11 percent below Friday's close. Oracle's stock fell 8.8 percent. Today, per Bloomberg Law, Meta is marketing at least 20 billion dollars of bonds. And at the same Hong Kong summit, Blackstone's Jon Gray said, quote, "There's going to be more equity needed."

Meanwhile, oil had its biggest drop in six months on Monday, per Bloomberg. Then, overnight, the US struck Iran's Qeshm Island. Polls start closing at 5 Central tonight. Treasury's refunding is tomorrow morning. Payrolls are Friday.
