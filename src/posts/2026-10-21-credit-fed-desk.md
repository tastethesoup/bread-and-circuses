Good morning. It's Wednesday, October 21st, and you are listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Yesterday looked calm on the surface. The S and P 500 rose 0.28 percent. The Dow rose 1.02 percent. The Nasdaq Composite was flat.

Bonds rallied at the long end. The ten-year Treasury closed at 5.12 percent, down 3 basis points. The twenty-year and the 30-year each fell 6, right into today's twenty-year auction.

But Monday's junk-bond spreads are now in, and the bottom of the market kept cracking. Bonds rated CCC and lower reached 13.10 percent. That's above 13 for the first time in the stretch I track. And per Bloomberg, default-risk gauges for some of the biggest tech companies hit fresh highs on Tuesday. Meanwhile, pending home sales fell to a record low.

This morning is brighter. SpaceX posted a quarterly profit of 4.4 billion dollars, against 1.88 billion expected. Korea's Kospi rose 3.3 percent, and futures are up. Ken Griffin of Citadel was asked whether AI is a bubble. His answer: "Almost certainly." Tonight, Meta and Tesla report.

Matt Levine of Bloomberg Opinion put today's question in a headline: "Someone Has to Take the AI Risk." So who?
