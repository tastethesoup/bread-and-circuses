Good morning. It's Tuesday, November 10th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

At three o'clock this morning, Central time, Fed Governor Christopher Waller stood up at a conference in London and said this: "Let's just say it. September's inflation data were bad. There was no silver lining."

Then he drew a line. If October's core CPI came in around 0.3 percent or higher, he said, "a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting."

At 7:30, the number landed. Core CPI rose 0.3 percent, or 0.296 unrounded, per Nick Timiraos of the Wall Street Journal. The third straight month at that pace. Headline rose 0.4. Both beat forecasts.

Timiraos's read, on X: "At a minimum, today's report is likely to lead officials to conclude that they will need to do more next year, not less."

That's our theme. More next year, not less.

Per CNBC, citing CME FedWatch, a December hike is now close to fully priced, with an 11 percent chance of a half point, up from zero a week ago. The two-year jumped more than 12 basis points, and the ten-year auction is at noon.

In credit, CoreWeave reports tonight, and two regional Fed banks have started asking private lenders what they're lending.
