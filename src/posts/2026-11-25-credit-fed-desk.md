Good morning. It's Wednesday, November 25th, the day before Thanksgiving. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

At 7:30 Central this morning, three numbers landed at once. Core PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose to 3.3 percent over twelve months. Economists expected 3.2. Third-quarter growth was revised up to 4.3 percent. And jobless claims fell to 189,000.

Stock futures, already lower, fell further after the data. Per Reuters at 8:34 Eastern, S&P 500 futures were down about 2.1 percent, and Nasdaq 100 futures down about 3.1.

And overnight, per Reuters, the cost of insuring against default at Oracle, Alphabet, SpaceX and CoreWeave hit records.

Tracy Alloway put it this way in Tuesday's Bloomberg Odd Lots newsletter: "it looks very likely that the next dollar of AI investment is about to get more expensive to fund."

So here's today's question. The Fed's gauge says money isn't getting cheaper. AI-linked credit says it's already getting more expensive. Who lends the next dollar, and at what price? A question, not a prediction.
