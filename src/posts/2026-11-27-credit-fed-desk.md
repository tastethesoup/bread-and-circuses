Good morning. It's Friday, November 27th, the day after Thanksgiving. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk. It's a half day: stocks close at noon Central, and bonds at 1.

Start with Wednesday. Early in the session, the Nasdaq was down as much as 4.6 percent and the S&P 500 down 3.2, per CNBC. By the close, the Nasdaq was down just 0.62 percent, the S&P 0.25. The Dow had been down more than 1,100 points. It finished up 164.

And Treasuries rallied, on the same morning core inflation came in hot. In Wednesday's Bloomberg Odd Lots newsletter, Joe Weisenthal passed along a point from Nomura's Charlie McElligott: "Treasuries are working as a hedge again."

That evening, Nick Timiraos of the Wall Street Journal posted a new article. Its public summary line reads: "The AI-driven market rout shows how much higher the bar has risen for the central bank to step in."

So here's today's question. If the Fed's bar for stepping in is that high, who steps in instead? Bond buyers? Bargain-hunting credit funds? Or nobody? And at what price? A question, not a prediction.
