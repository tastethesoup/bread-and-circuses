Good morning. It's Friday, November 20th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

At Bloomberg's Global Credit Forum in London this week, Michela Bariletti, the chief credit officer at Standard Life, asked the question of the week. Quote: "How do you predict AI? You can't. The question for these new capex opportunities in the long term is if one's AI growth story is wrong by 20 to 30 percent, who is going to ultimately pay the bill?"

On Thursday, the bond market wrote down one answer, with a price on it. SpaceX sold 32 billion dollars of debt to buy Nvidia chips. The borrowing sits in a separate trust, off SpaceX's balance sheet. SpaceX leases the chips, and it has to pay, in the rating agency's words, "hell or high water." Investors charged 8.259 percent on the biggest piece, when the average investment-grade bond yields 5.95.

The week in one breath. Junk spreads went from 323 basis points last Friday to 352 on Thursday. The ten-year went from 5.15 to 5.04, and this morning it's around 4.98. Under five.

So that's our theme. Who pays the bill? When AI borrowing moves into trusts, leases and private funds, who pays if the story comes in 20 to 30 percent short? A question, not a prediction.
