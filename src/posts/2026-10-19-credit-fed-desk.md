Good morning. It's Monday, October 19th, and you are listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

The Fed has gone quiet. Its blackout started Saturday. From now until after the October 28th decision, there are no official Fed speakers. None.

So the markets get the microphone. On Friday, the ten-year Treasury closed at 5.09 percent. That's down 15 basis points on the week. A week of hot inflation data, and the long end rallied anyway.

Credit is still sore. High-yield spreads eased a touch on Thursday, to 323 basis points from 325. But the riskiest junk bonds, rated CCC and lower, are still near 12.8 percent.

Over the weekend, France saw its worst street violence in years. This morning, the French ten-year yield jumped 8 basis points, to 4.88 percent. The CAC in Paris fell 1.5 percent.

Here at home, futures are trying to bounce. Nasdaq 100 futures are up about 1.1 percent.

And John Authers of Bloomberg Opinion ties last week's turn in stocks to stress in credit, specifically around AI debt. His marker: CoreWeave's debt yielding around 15 percent.
