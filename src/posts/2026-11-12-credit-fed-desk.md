Good morning. It's Thursday, November 12th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

This morning, Nvidia's board announced that Jensen Huang "has resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer, and a member of the Board, effective immediately." Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim CEO. On the change itself, we're sticking to the company's release.

Why lead a credit show with it? Because CNBC says Nvidia's commitments to the rest of the industry have earned it "the nickname the 'central bank of AI.'" And this morning, per a relay of ICE data, the cost of insuring Nvidia's own debt ticked up toward its highs of the month.

John Authers, in Points of Return last night, named the problem. Nvidia, he wrote, "had badly underestimated the key-man risk" it was taking in its chief executive.

That's our theme today. Key-man risk. What happens when the one person, or the one seat, a structure leans on is gone.

It runs through the Fed, too. Nick Timiraos of the Wall Street Journal wrote on Sunday that the new Senate leaves the President with the Fed chair of his choosing, "but one seat short of a majority."

Also this morning. Nebius says up to 85 percent of next year's build-out will be covered by customer prepayments and asset-backed financing. The riskiest junk bonds widened while the broad index tightened. The ten-year is back above 5 percent in the first cash session since Tuesday, with the 30-year auction at noon. And the Cleveland Fed's Hammack, per headlines, wants "smaller, steadier" hikes.
