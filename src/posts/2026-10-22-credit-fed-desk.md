Good morning. It's Thursday, October 22nd, and you are listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Wednesday ended green. The S and P 500 rose 0.58 percent, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.05 percent.

Then the reports came out. Meta's revenue grew 26 percent. It narrowed its 2026 spending outlook to 135 to 145 billion dollars, lifting the bottom of the range. It spent more cash than it brought in for the quarter. And it announced a brand new class of nonvoting stock. Tesla went the other way and cut its spending plan to about 20 billion dollars. Lam Research is down more than 9 percent on weak guidance.

So this morning, stock futures are down about 1 percent, and Nasdaq futures closer to 2. The ten-year yield is back to about 5.07 percent, per Bloomberg.

Tuesday's junk-bond spreads are in. Bonds rated CCC and lower reached 13.27 percent, a new high in the stretch I track. The twenty-year auction came in soft, at the highest yield for a twenty-year auction since 1986. Jobless claims came in at 194,000, the lowest since July. And Moody's reviews France tomorrow.

Santiago Mateo of CaixaBank Asset Management put the mood in six words: "Capex alone is no longer enough."
