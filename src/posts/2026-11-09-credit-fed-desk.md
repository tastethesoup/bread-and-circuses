Good morning. It's Monday, November 9th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Here's today in one line, from Daniel Roberts, co-CEO of the AI cloud company IREN. He said it last week, and Bloomberg quoted him this morning: "The debt markets are open. They're just more selective."

That's our theme. Open, but more selective.

The junk bond index settled down Friday. The high-yield spread came in to 342 basis points, from 348. But the borrowers at the center of the AI buildout did not settle down. Per CNBC, some CoreWeave bonds yield more than 16 percent, and the company reports tomorrow. And per Reuters, some banks are now reluctant to fund new AI deals, even with guarantees from the sector's biggest names.

The Fed is getting choosier too. A December hike is back to roughly four in five, per secondary relays of CME FedWatch. A Fed staff note says oil feeds into core inflation more than it used to. And Nick Timiraos of the Wall Street Journal says a Democratic Senate may leave the Fed's board just as it is.

The three-year auction stopped at 4.627 percent, a half-basis-point tail, with a soft 2.53 bid-to-cover. CPI is tomorrow. And Nvidia was down about four and a half percent before the open, per Bloomberg, after news involving its chief executive. Facts only, in the stocks segment.
