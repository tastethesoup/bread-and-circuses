Good morning. It's Tuesday, November 24th. You're listening to the Credit and Fed Desk.

Monday was the best day for the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq since April 9th, 2025. The S&P rose 4.73 percent. The Nasdaq rose 6.51 percent.

That same afternoon, the president of the New York Fed, John Williams, gave a speech in the Bronx. Talking about inflation, he ended with this: "There is still work to do to get that done."

Now hold that line up against the bottom of the credit market. On Friday, the spread on CCC bonds, the lowest-rated junk debt, hit 14.02 percent. That's the highest in the three years of FRED data I keep on file. And this morning, America's Car-Mart, a subprime used-car dealer and lender, filed for Chapter 11.

So here's today's question. Monday's rally came from the top of the market. Did anything change at the bottom of the credit stack? Or, in Williams' words, is there still work to do? A question, not a prediction.

And this morning, futures are lower, and per CNBC, all three major averages opened in the red. Monday was one day. This morning is one morning. Bloomberg's Points of Return newsletter put it plainly overnight: Monday's bounce is, quote, "for now, still only a bounce."
